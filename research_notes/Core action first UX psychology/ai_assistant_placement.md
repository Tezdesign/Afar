# AI Assistant and Natural-Language Search Placement in Consumer Travel/Search Products (2024-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Evidence labels: **[Published]** = study, vendor statement, or official page; **[Commentary]** = opinion/blog/press analysis; **[Unverified]** = could not confirm from a fetched source. Many product facts below come from press coverage, not the vendor's own page, and AI products change monthly, so every placement claim may be stale. Where a finding came only from a web-search summary (page not fetched in full), it is marked "(search summary)".

## 1. How do current travel products place AI (tab, floating button, search-box mode, full-screen chat, layer on results)? Is output editable or text-only?

### Takeaway
No single pattern dominates. Shipped patterns seen: (a) a top-level mode/tab beside Flights/Stays/Cars (Kayak AI Mode); (b) a free-text field added to the search form (Airbnb "What" box test); (c) a chat that returns visual cards and deep-links into the normal booking funnel (Booking.com, Skyscanner Savvy Search, Expedia/Booking/Skyscanner in ChatGPT); (d) AI that auto-applies ordinary filters (Booking.com Smart Filter, Kayak Ask Kayak); (e) a side-by-side chat plus workspace panel (Google Canvas). Most keep the traditional search/filters alive next to the AI. Whether the AI's interpretation is shown as editable chips is mostly undocumented in sources I could reach.

Official debut Wed 15 Oct 2025; soft-launched on mobile web a few weeks earlier. The Skift text did not describe result layout, cards or filters. [Published/press] — [Skift, 15 Oct 2025](https://skift.com/2025/10/15/kayak-ai-mode-natural-language-search/)

### Inferences
- The two most "first-class" placements seen are Kayak's top-nav mode and Airbnb's search-form field; both put AI at the point of intent entry rather than in a floating bubble. Neither is a global floating button.
- Hand-off to the existing funnel (cards then deep-link, or Smart Filter that sets normal filters) is the dominant monetisation-safe pattern; fully in-chat transaction is the exception (Priceline Penny, Perplexity Instant Buy, Google hotel test).
- Chat inside ChatGPT is a distribution channel, not an in-app placement choice, so it tells us little about in-app placement.

## 2. Evidence on natural-language search that shows interpretation as editable filters/chips versus free-form chat answers

### Takeaway
There is little direct published evidence comparing "interpretation shown as chips" to "chat answer". What exists points the same way: NN/g (small qualitative study, 2026) and Amadeus (small studies, 2026) recommend pairing conversation with filters and structured output because users want control and verification for choose/buy tasks; Nielsen advocates hybrid intent-plus-GUI. Product evidence of chips specifically (Airbnb, Zillow, Google Flights) is not documented in sources I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g remote observational study (Maria Rosala, Josh Brown, 27 Feb 2026), roughly 9 participants (count inferred from "6 of 9"), real tasks including "Choose" tasks like trip planning: users turned to AI for vague ideas, multiple constraints, fewer clicks, review summaries, cross-site comparison; they returned to search for validation, exact prices, high-stakes choices. Participants valued control "over the search process"; citations did not fully resolve trust because users could not tell which source backed which claim. The article's design implications (inferred from findings, not a formal list): combine conversation with filters/comparison tables, allow structured/table output, traceable sourcing, route prices to authoritative sources, improve discoverability (it notes Google's AI Mode was underused partly due to low discoverability). [Published, small n] — [NN/g, AI Search Infoseeking](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-search-infoseeking/)
- Amadeus engineering blog (2026, vendor research, small samples): internal survey Feb 2026 of 30 US travellers, only 13% chose "I prefer a chatbot over a regular website"; larger Mar 2026 survey (n=1,031) 70% comfortable with "interactive chat" vs 66% "text chat"; cited Google Generative UI research found generated UI preferred over raw LLM text in 97% of pairwise comparisons (Elo 1736 vs 1174) and over markdown in 90.6% (general prompts, not travel); a May 2026 prototype with six leisure travellers rebooking a flight let them type or use generated UI components. Cited 2021 student study: chatbot scored higher on open-ended tasks, the familiar interface on closed fixed-parameter tasks, and the chatbot needed more cognitive effort. Recommendation: conversation for ambiguity/exploration, validated structured components once intent is clear ("decisional UX"). [Published, vendor, small n] — [Amadeus](https://amadeus.com/en/engineering-blog/articles/ai-chatbot-travel-chat-generated-ui-decisional-ux)
- Academic: a 2025 study of 22 participants using a combined search + AI-chat interface concluded GenAI is neither a search panacea nor a major regression (search summary of arXiv abstract) — [Blending Queries and Conversations, arXiv 2504.05156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05156)
- Nielsen argues GUIs "have superior usability because they show people what can be done," so hybrids of intent-based prompting plus graphical elements are needed; cites Grammarly's typed-request-plus-buttons as a step in that direction. [Commentary by an expert; admits no large-scale writing studies] — [UX Tigers, Articulation Barrier (May 2023, updated Apr 2025)](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/ai-articulation-barrier)
- Zillow: natural-language search typed in the search bar since Jan 2023, expanded Sept 2024 with voice prompts on commute, POIs, affordability, school access. Sources I reached do not say whether parsed criteria are shown as removable chips. — [The Title Report, Jan 2023](https://www.thetitlereport.com/TTR/Articles/87414.aspx); [Inman, 4 Sept 2024](https://inman.com/2024/09/04/zillow-unfiltered-portal-giant-adds-ai-fueled-natural-language-feature)
- Perplexity Pro Search: plans multi-step sub-queries, may ask follow-up questions, answers carry numbered citations; it is an opt-in toggle per query. Step-by-step visibility details not confirmed. (search summary) — [Perplexity FAQ](https://hub-prod.perplexity.ai/hub/faq/how-does-perplexity-work)
- Booking.com Smart Filter (applies normal filters from a description) and Ask Kayak (filters by typing) are shipped examples of the "interpretation becomes ordinary filters" pattern, but whether the user can see/edit them is unverified (see Q1).

### Inferences
- The "translate to filters, then show them" pattern is consistent with every control/verification finding above, but I found no published A/B or usability test isolating it. Treat as design reasoning, not proven.
- For high-stakes "Choose/Buy" steps (price, dates, passengers), evidence favours structured display; for early exploration, free-form is favoured.

### Gaps
- No study found directly testing chips-as-interpretation vs chat-answer.
- NN/g sample size not stated in the text I read; Amadeus samples are small and vendor-run.
- Could not verify Zillow, Airbnb or Google Flights chip behaviour.

## 3. Usability evidence on chat as primary interface vs search/forms (articulation barrier, discoverability, blank-prompt, suggested prompts, structured controls alongside chat)

### Takeaway
Strongest named concept is Nielsen's "articulation barrier", which is mostly expert argument built on literacy statistics, not a direct test. Empirical studies (NN/g 2026, Amadeus 2026, 2021 student study) support a split: chat for open/vague tasks, structured UI for fixed-parameter or high-stakes ones, with discoverability a known weakness.

### Cited Findings
- Articulation barrier (Nielsen): prompt-driven interfaces need clear written articulation; he cites OECD PIAAC data that about half of adults in the US/Germany are low-literacy, and estimates (his own guess, unmeasured) that under 20% of people, best guess 10%, can use advanced prompt-driven AI well. He states no large-scale writing studies exist. [Commentary; literacy numbers are Published OECD data, articulation claim is unverified opinion] — [UX Tigers, May 2023 / updated Apr 2025](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/ai-articulation-barrier)
- Nielsen's "Prompt Augmentation" (Mar 2025) lists six patterns to help users: style galleries, prompt rewrite, targeted prompt rewrite, related prompts, prompt builders, parametrization; also suggests voice conversation may help less-literate users. [Commentary] — [Substack: Prompt Augmentation](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/prompt-augmentation)
- Intent-based outcome specification (Nielsen, first stated May 2023): AI as a third UI paradigm where the user states the goal and the system picks the steps; his Chicago example notes underspecification (budget, fixed meetings, whether the AI may buy tickets or only prepare options), so UX must help users express what the system may assume and execute. He also says it does not remove usability issues and the articulation barrier remains. [Commentary] — [Substack: AI Is First New UI Paradigm in 60 Years](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ai-is-first-new-ui-paradigm-in-60); [Intent by Discovery](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/intent-ux)
- NN/g 2026 study: chat helped when users were unsure how to phrase a query or the space was unfamiliar; AI discoverability was a barrier (Google AI Mode underused). See Q2.
- Amadeus: only 13% (n=30) preferred a chatbot over a regular website; interactive chat edged text chat 70% vs 66% (n=1,031). See Q2.
- Skyscanner said Savvy Search imagery plus descriptions came from traveller testing; the product gives a constrained output (up to three destinations) instead of an open answer. [Published/press] — [Business Traveller, May 2024](https://www.businesstraveller.com/business-travel/2024/05/14/skyscanner-introduces-savvy-search-its-innovative-ai-travel-planning-tool/)
- Amershi et al., CHI 2019: 18 human-AI interaction guidelines from 160+ candidate recommendations, validated with 49 practitioners across 20 products; grouped by initial interaction, during interaction, when wrong, over time. — [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)
- Google PAIR Guidebook "Errors + Graceful Failure": design for misunderstandings; define "error" from user's perspective; give users paths forward and control after failure. — [PAIR chapter](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/errors-failing/)

### Inferences
- The literature does not contain a travel-specific test of "blank prompt vs suggested prompts". The suggested-prompt benefit is inferred from Nielsen's prompt-augmentation proposals (commentary) and the NN/g discoverability finding.
- Chat suits "I don't know the destination" moments; the origin/dates/passengers step is a closed-parameter task where forms did better in the cited 2021 study.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on travel-booking chat vs form completion time/errors in 2024-2026 other than Amadeus' small ones.
- Nielsen's numbers are estimates; do not present as measured.
- NN/g's own page titled for "articulation barrier" not found; the term traces to Nielsen's personal UX Tigers site.

## 4. Voice interfaces: when preferred vs text (public settings, noise, privacy, Arabic dialect accuracy, error recovery, transaction confirmation risk)

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and not travel-specific. Published work shows privacy wariness rises when speaking sensitive content in public, native-language fluency can make speaking easier, Arabic ASR varies strongly by dialect and test conditions, and voice-only confirmation/recovery is weaker than visual confirmation. No source found tested voice confirmation of a travel purchase.

### Cited Findings
- Privacy: survey of smartphone voice assistant users found people more cautious sharing private vs non-private information, amplified in public locations and with conspicuous input like speaking. [Published, academic, older] — [Synapse record](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2051626970) (search summary; full methods not read)
- Google Assistant study: voice raised perceived social presence only for less sensitive health queries; device type made no difference. [Published, academic, 2019] — [NJIT record](https://researchwith.njit.edu/en/publications/hey-google-can-i-ask-you-something-in-private-the-effects-of-moda/)
- A 2026 design study found participants worried about speaking commands in public but valued voice's expressiveness and said native-language fluency can make speaking easier than typing for multilingual users (domain: immersive network visualization, not travel). — [arXiv 2607.26526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26526)
- Arabic ASR: 2021 benchmark found a human-machine gap in WER of about 3.5% absolute on average — [arXiv 2101.08454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.08454); a 2025 dialect dataset study reported highest error rates for Algerian and Sudanese dialects and lowest for Palestinian and Saudi — [Bulbul dataset, arXiv 2608.21950](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21950); a small study (13 Madurese learners of Arabic) found Whisper 84.0% vs Google STT 61.8% accuracy — [Al-Ibbaa journal](https://ejournal.uinmadura.ac.id/index.php/alibbaa/article/view/24450). A vendor (Sestek) claims near-zero WER on Egyptian-dialect call audio after fine-tuning; self-reported, commercial interest, treat as unverified — [Sestek](https://www.sestek.com/blog/speech-recognition-accuracy-test-2024-arabic-edition). Most are clean/recorded audio, not noisy public settings. Search summaries; I did not read full papers.
- Voice confirmation: a 2004 Interspeech comparison found a final confirmation with fewer turns preferred when there is no error, and explicit confirmation preferred when an error occurs — [ISCA Interspeech 2004](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2004/sagawa04_interspeech.html); an ISI study found users who discover errors via implicit confirmation are less likely to recover and take longer than when the system rejects/reprompts — [ISI publication](https://www.isi.edu/results/publications/17917/analysis-of-user-behavior-under-error-conditions-in-spoken-dialogs); older adults strongly preferred smart screens over voice-only because voice-only output loads working memory and visual confirmation helped — [CareHub review](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69b96e1a4a38ebb229bb2778) (low-grade source).
- Product statements: Gemini Agent says to take control of the browser and type payment/sign-in details yourself rather than put them in the chat thread — [Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16596215). Airbnb said it plans to add voice support to its AI customer service — [Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/airbnb-ai-first-expands-customer-service-agent-us/757237/). Zillow added voice prompts to natural-language search in 2024 — [Inman](https://inman.com/2024/09/04/zillow-unfiltered-portal-giant-adds-ai-fueled-natural-language-feature).

### Inferences
- A voice-originated booking should likely end with a visual confirmation of the key fields (route, dates, price, traveller) because implicit/aural confirmation is the weakest recovery path in the cited work. This is design reasoning, not a tested result for travel.
- Arabic dialect performance should be tested per target dialect and in noise before promising voice parity; the published spread across dialects is large.

### Gaps
- No study of voice vs text for travel search specifically; no study of Tunisian/Maghrebi dialect ASR located.
- No source found on voice-confirmed transactions in shipped travel products.
- Several sources are old (2004-2019) and non-travel.

## 5. Agent action patterns: confirm / preview / undo / activity log in shipped products, and stated reasons

### Takeaway
Shipped agents consistently gate irreversible or sensitive steps (purchase, sending, publishing, payment/sign-in) behind explicit user confirmation or hand-over, and cite prompt-injection and error risk as the reason. Booking flows in travel are mostly "AI assembles, user confirms on a final review page", often on the merchant's page. Undo and activity-log patterns are barely documented in what I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic Claude for Chrome (25 Aug 2025; updated Nov and Dec 2025): "Claude asks users before taking high-risk actions like publishing, purchasing, or sharing personal data"; per-site access control; blocked categories include financial services, adult, pirated; stated reason: prompt injection. Red-team: 123 test cases / 29 attack scenarios, 23.6% attack success without mitigations in autonomous mode, 11.2% with them; a browser-specific attack set went 35.7% to 0%. [Published, vendor] — [Anthropic/Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-chrome)
- Claude in Chrome help (later): permission modes "Manually approve" (formerly "Ask before acting"), "Automatically approve", "Skip all approvals"; classifiers check incoming content and each action; per-domain permissions are the primary protection; a vendor figure of under 0.08% attack success on internal testing (not comparable to the 2025 figures). (search summary) — [Claude Help: use Claude in Chrome safely](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902428-use-claude-in-chrome-safely); [permissions guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902446-manage-claude-in-chrome-permissions)
- Google Gemini Agent (help page): for every task involving web browsing Gemini asks for confirmation first; designed to ask for review before sending communications, modifying data, purchases, submitting forms, signing in; user can "Take over task" in a live view; Gemini may pause for passwords/payment; do not enter payment/sign-in in the chat. Limits: AI Pro/Ultra only, not available in EEA, Switzerland, UK, Nigeria; up to 15 concurrent tasks. [Published, vendor] — [Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16596215)
- Google AI Mode agentic booking (Nov 2025): restaurant reservations broadly in US; tickets and local appointments for Labs opt-ins; flights/hotels "not yet" at that date; the demo has AI list matching hotels, then asks the user to confirm details, books, shows a confirmation. [Press] — [BGR, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.bgr.com/2028290/google-flight-deals-ai-mode-canvas-travel/). Later reports (search summary, lower confidence): hotel booking test uses "Continue on Google" with Google Pay and a cancellation-policy review; Gemini/AI Mode checkout (Jan 2026) pre-fills a final review page and, for price tracking, the user confirms and taps "buy for me" — [9to5Google, 11 Jan 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/01/11/gemini-ai-mode-checkout/); [Deep Arrival, Aug 2026 hotel test](https://deeparrival.com/news/google-agentic-hotel-booking-us-test-august-2026/)
- OpenAI agent: help center lists user confirmations for high-impact actions, "watch mode" (user must supervise on certain sites) and says safeguards do not remove all risk; the help page also states ChatGPT agent is no longer available and points to other products (status changes; fetch of the English page returned 403, so this is from a search summary of the fr-CA page) — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/fr-ca/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent). An academic paper reports in watch mode the agent stops taking screenshots and frames the trade-off as triggering too rarely (misses sensitive content) vs too often (interrupts) — [arXiv 2512.00742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.00742). OpenAI's Operator launch page returned 403; "takeover mode" claims are **[Unverified]** here.
- Handover research: a study on user/agent control handover found user-only triggering left people unable to intervene effectively, while agent-always-asks created confirmation burden; both sides needing the ability to trigger handover was the conclusion (search summary) — [arXiv 2509.10723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10723)
- Perplexity Instant Buy with PayPal (25 Nov 2025, US): product cards in chat, Instant Buy button, first-time PayPal link, user confirms purchase in the chat; PayPal handles payment. (search summary) — [PayPal explainer](https://history.paypal.com/us/money-hub/article/how-paypal-powers-ai-shopping); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/paypal-perplexity-instant-buy-shopping/)
- Travel in-chat booking: Expedia/Booking.com in ChatGPT hand off to their own sites to complete (Q1); Priceline Penny reported able to book/change/cancel in the prompt box (2023, Fast Company). Confirm/undo specifics for Penny not documented in sources reached.

## 6. Global floating assistant button vs assistant as tab vs assistant inside search box

### Takeaway
No controlled study comparing the three placements was found. Evidence is design-system guidance, forum complaints and product choices: floating buttons maximise reach but risk occluding content; dedicated tab/mode is predictable but needs a deliberate visit; search-box placement fits question-like, intent-entry use. Shipped travel products lean toward mode/tab (Kayak) and search-box field (Airbnb).

### Cited Findings
- Search summary of design guidance: floating button suited to an assistant needed everywhere; bottom-right is conventional for help/chat widgets; SAP guidance warns floating panels can cover important content and suggests a minimised state that keeps the conversation alive; users in forum threads report floating AI buttons overlapping UI (Discourse, ClickUp); a ClickUp thread had users saying a tab felt more integrated and was used more (anecdotal). [Commentary / vendor guidance] — [SAP Design System: AI placement](https://www.sap.com/design-system/btp/patterns/generative-ai/ai-placement/usage); [LogRocket: AI UI placement](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/ai-ui-placement/); [ClickUp feedback thread](https://feedback.clickup.com/feature-requests/p/feedback-on-floating-ai-tab-vs-dedicated-section-in-clickup); [Discourse Meta thread](https://meta.discourse.org/t/misplaced-ai-docs-overlay-button/366138)
- A placement framework (daily.dev summary) frames the choice as discoverability vs interruption, driven by how central AI is, how often it is needed, and category conventions. [Commentary] — [daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/where-should-ai-go-in-your-ui-a-ux-guide-to-ai-feature-placement-zgg2sjwtt)
- Chrome was reported testing an "Ask anything" AI Mode field replacing the standard search prompt; Google said it is an early prototype behind a flag. [Press] — [Windows Report](https://windowsreport.com/chrome-tests-a-floating-ai-mode-search-bar-for-desktop/)
- Product statements: Kayak made AI Mode a top-nav button beside Flights/Stays/Cars (Skift 2025); Airbnb described the "What" box as phase one before a unified assistant spanning search, support and messaging (Skift 2025); Google's AI Mode in search was found underused partly due to low discoverability in the NN/g study (2026); Priceline/Skift describe Penny as replacing separate searches, filters and tabs (Skift 2026). See Q1 for links.

### Inferences
- If the assistant is a core entry point, mode/tab or search-box placement matches what Kayak and Airbnb shipped; a global floating button is better justified for context-aware help (e.g. "ask about this flight") than for first-time search, but no travel-specific evidence supports this.
- The NN/g discoverability finding is the closest published evidence that hidden or secondary AI entry points get underused.

### Gaps
- No usability study or A/B result comparing the three placements.
- No Mobbin/Growth.design/Lenny's teardown located for any travel AI placement.
- Whether any major OTA uses a global floating assistant button: not found. **[Unverified]**
